Video comparison study: information and consent

What you will do. You will watch short computer-generated videos of people moving and, for each pair, answer a few questions about which video is better. The study takes about 10 minutes. Please use a desktop or laptop computer; the videos have no sound.

Purpose. The study evaluates methods that generate videos of a person from a photo and a reference video. The people in the videos were generated by AI methods from photos and videos.

Data we collect. Your answers, the time spent on each comparison, your browser type, and an anonymous participant code (your Prolific ID if you came from Prolific, used to pay you; otherwise a random code made by this page), used to remove duplicate submissions. We do not collect your name, email, IP address or any other personal information. Participant codes are removed before any data are shared or published; the anonymised answers may be published as part of a research paper.

Voluntary participation. Taking part is voluntary. You may stop at any time by closing this page and returning the study on Prolific. To withdraw your data after taking part, contact the study organiser through Prolific with your Prolific ID.

Risks. There are no known risks beyond normal computer use.

Thank you. You can close this page. Please return the study on Prolific; you will not be penalised.

  1. Each screen shows, from left to right: a photo (a person in a scene; the first frame), a reference video (a person performing a motion), and two generated videos, Video A and Video B. The text instruction is on top.
  2. Video A and Video B were made by two different video editing methods. Each one makes the person in the photo perform the motion of the reference video, changed the way the text instruction asks (for example "walk backwards" or "keep both arms down"). When the instruction only asks to perform the reference motion, nothing should change: the video should copy the reference motion (those screens have no separate question about the instruction). A good video:
    • does what the text instruction asks;
    • apart from that change, moves like the reference performer (same steps, rhythm, posture, arm swing);
    • keeps the person, clothes and scene of the photo, with the person facing the same way as in the photo (front, side or back). If the reference performer turns during the video, the person should make the same turn: that is correct, not a change of view;
    • looks natural, without distorted bodies, flicker or other visual errors.
  3. The three videos play together in a loop; Play from start restarts them together. Answer each question with A better, About the same or B better. If you are unsure, make your best judgment. If the two videos look the same, choose About the same.
  4. Next becomes active once every question is answered; Previous goes back. Your progress is saved in this browser.
